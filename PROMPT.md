## 🔴🔴🔴 LEAD WITH THIS — a 4.9 from 257 reviews, locked inside Google Maps

This is a first-website build. **There is no defect list, because there is no site.** The argument is opportunity, and here the opportunity has a number on it.

**PureFX holds 4.9 out of 5 across 257 reviews.** For a mobile detailer that is exceptional — not good, exceptional. Two hundred and fifty-seven people went back to a map pin and typed something. And that entire asset exists in exactly one place: inside Google Maps, where it works only for the person who is already looking at Google Maps.

**What that rating cannot currently do:**
- It cannot rank them for a search. A rating is not a page; Google has nothing of theirs to rank.
- It cannot be shown to someone comparing three quotes in a text thread. There is no link to send.
- It cannot appear in a share, a referral, an Instagram bio tap, a flyer QR code or a quote email — because all of those need a destination, and they do not have one.

**That is reputation that cannot travel.** It is the single most valuable thing this business owns and it is the single least portable. Say it in those words.

**Second thread — twenty service areas and not one page.** Hiram, Canton, Dallas, Smyrna, Acworth, Atlanta, Austell, Emerson, Roswell, Kennesaw, Mableton, Marietta, Alpharetta, Cartersville, Douglasville, Holly Springs, Sandy Springs, Lithia Springs, Powder Springs, Villa Rica. A Google Business Profile ranks a business **where it is registered**. It cannot rank them for *"mobile detailing Alpharetta"*, *"paint correction Marietta"* or *"window tinting Kennesaw"*, because those searches need **pages** and there are none. For a business whose whole operational advantage is that it drives to the customer, local search across the radius **is** the growth lever — and it is missing entirely. See §Location pages, and read the honesty warning there before quoting a number of pages: **eight genuinely distinct pages beat twenty thin ones**, and twenty find-and-replace pages are worse than having none.

**Third thread — thirteen services, flat, with no packages and no prices.** Auto detailing, car waxing, engine detailing, headlight polishing, seat shampooing, wheel washing, paint correction, interior vacuuming, clay bar treatment, full body wash, interior scenting, steam cleaning, window tinting. That list runs from *vacuuming a floor mat* to *multi-stage paint correction* to *installing film on glass* — wildly different jobs at wildly different prices — and it is presented as one undifferentiated row of chips. A buyer reading it has no way to work out what they would be spending, or even which of these things are the same visit. **This is what the design system is for.** See §Art direction.

**Fourth, for later in the conversation, not the opener:** a customer states in a review that they **booked through an Instagram DM**. That works until it doesn't. A DM queue is unsearchable, unassignable, invisible to anyone who isn't already following them, and it dies the day the person who reads the inbox is elbow-deep in a door card. Raise it late and as a capacity question, not a criticism.

### 🧨 WALK-AWAY

**None.** A fully mobile detailer holding 4.9 from 257 reviews across twenty metro Atlanta areas, offering thirteen services and taking three payment methods, with no website at all — that is a good business with a missing asset, which is the best kind of lead.

⚠️ **Three conditions, not walk-aways:**
1. **Tint.** If they want VLT figures, "limo tint", "darkest legal" or dark-glass hero photography without supplying their certification and the statutory limits in writing, the tint section ships as **a service name and one neutral sentence**. Alex says why, in writing, once. We do not advertise an illegal vehicle modification because it converts well.
2. **Reviews.** If they want review text on the page before L8 is settled, the review surface ships as empty labelled frames. The rating is the pitch, not the mockup.
3. **Location pages.** If nobody will resource real content for twenty areas, we launch **eight to ten genuinely distinct pages** and add the rest as content arrives. ⛔ We do not ship twenty templated pages to hit a number.

### 📌 Why these apply

**Window tint is the one place on this site where bad copy is advertising an offence.** Georgia fixes statutory minimum visible-light-transmission limits for vehicle windows and requires installers to provide certification. A page that says a number, names a shade, or shows near-black front side glass is making a representation about what PureFX will install — and if that representation sits below the limit, the page is advertising an illegal modification in their name, with their phone number on it. The figures also change, and they are not ours to assert: they come from the installer, in writing, which is also how we find out whether their certification is current. **This is not legal advice; it is a rule about what we will render.**

**257 reviews at 4.9 is an asset and a trap in the same breath.** The figure is real and verifiable, so Alex should say it out loud, repeatedly, in the meeting. But the reviews themselves belong to the customers who wrote them. Lifting a stranger's words onto a speculative mockup for a business that has not signed is not ours to do — and Google's own terms govern how reviews may be displayed off-platform. Building the review surface as finished-but-empty makes exactly the right argument: *here is the shelf your best asset is missing from.*

**Steam and scent are the two services most likely to attract a regulated verb.** The moment copy says steam *sanitises*, *kills* or *disinfects*, or that scenting makes a cabin *healthier* or *allergen-free*, it becomes a pesticidal and health claim under **FIFRA** and the **FTC Act** — and a device marketed with those claims may require EPA registration. The honest description is better copy anyway: hot water extraction on fabric, and odour treatment. Nobody buying a seat shampoo needed the word "hospital".

**Paint correction has a physical ceiling.** Clear coat is finite. A defect deeper than the remaining clear coat cannot be polished out, and water-spot etching — which a review specifically describes — is the textbook case. "Reduces the appearance of" is both honest and standard trade language.

**Engine bays and headlights are cosmetic services dressed up as functional ones.** "Runs cooler", "better resale", "see further at night" are objective claims needing substantiation; none of them is why anybody buys either service. They buy them because the bay looks filthy and the lenses look yellow.

**"Open 24 hours, seven days" is a GBP artefact.** On a profile it signals contactability. On a website it reads as either a mistake or an invitation to call at 3am, and it undermines everything else on the page. Their real late availability is probably a genuine selling point — which is a reason to find out what it actually is, not to print the Google setting.

**Water is two separate questions and PureFX has answered neither.** Whether they need the customer's tap and outlet determines which properties they can work at. Where the rinse water ends up is a stormwater question under local Georgia ordinances and NPDES rules, and it determines which properties will *let* them work. Framing containment as eco-marketing invites a Green Guides problem; framing it as commercial capability wins fleet and property-management jobs.

**Four names in reviews are not a team page.** Devin, Cooper, Josiah and Ray may be staff, may be contractors, may include a customer we misread, and one of them is spelled two ways in public. Publishing a real person's name on a website is a decision they get to make.

**No minors, and none of their photos.** Their GBP and Instagram images are customers' cars — identifiable vehicles with readable plates, shot with no release for our use. And this is a trade where a stock photo of a child in a freshly cleaned back seat is one search away and completely off-limits. The car is the subject. It always was.

## Art direction — THE SERVICE MATRIX. Light, tabular, legible.

### The idea

**PureFX has thirteen services, no packages, and no prices. Their customer's actual problem is that they cannot tell what they would be buying.** So the design system is not an atmosphere — it is an **instrument**. The centrepiece of this site is a **real comparison matrix**: services down the left, package tiers across the top, vehicle sizes as a second axis, and a tick in every cell that applies.

**The system, stated as rules:**

- **High-key ground.** Near-white paper, near-black ink. Light is the default state of every surface; dark is used once, as punctuation, and never as a mood.
- **Everything aligns to a grid, visibly.** 1px hairlines, consistent gutters, tabular figures, baseline-aligned rows. The grid is not hidden — it is the ornament.
- **Generous whitespace as the premium signal.** Space, not gloss. A matrix only reads as precise if it has room.
- **The tick is the brand mark.** A single geometric glyph — a 2px stroked check in `--ink` — repeated across hundreds of cells. It is the most-repeated element on the site and therefore the logo-adjacent object. Where we do not know, the cell carries **`[INCLUDED? — CONFIRM]`** in mono, in `--graphite`, boxed in 1px `--rule`. ⛔ **Never a tick we were not given. Never a dash standing in for "no".**
- **Tabular alignment everywhere, not just in the table.** Service lists, area lists, the FAQ, the footer and the location-page index all use the same column rhythm. The site reads like a well-set specification document.
- **One dark surface, used once** — the quote form — so the page has a floor and the CTA has weight.

**Why this concept:** it solves their actual commercial problem rather than decorating around it; a finished-but-empty matrix **is the proposal**, because the client can see precisely where their own information goes; and it is structurally the opposite of every atmospheric detailing site in existence, including the one written in this folder an hour ago.

⛔ **Discipline, or it becomes a spreadsheet.** The matrix is **one section**, rendered beautifully: large type in the row headers, real breathing room, a sticky column header on scroll, and a genuine mobile treatment (stacked per-tier cards, not a pinch-to-zoom table). ⛔ No zebra striping. ⛔ No coloured cell fills. ⛔ No "savings" badges. ⛔ No heavy borders — hairlines only. ⛔ No icons substituting for service names.

The accent role — emphasis, CTA, the active state, the tick — is carried by **pure ink at full contrast**, which in a high-key system is the loudest thing available. ## Animations & gradients

The register is **precise, settled and quiet** — a well-set document coming into place, not a UI performing. ⛔ **Nothing glossy, nothing atmospheric, nothing that sweeps.**

- **Hairline draw — the signature motion.** On section reveal, the 1px `--rule` dividers **draw in left to right**, 320ms, `cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.3,1)`, then stop. ⚠️ It is a line being ruled, not a light sweeping — ⛔ **no bloom, no glow, no gradient on the line.** (⛔ This is explicitly *not* Glaze's wet-edge treatment: no 4px bloom, no specular, no opacity ramp.)
- **Matrix reveal.** Rows fade + rise 10px on scroll, **60ms stagger down the column**, 360ms. The sticky column header locks on scroll with no transition. ⛔ No cell-by-cell cascade, ⛔ no tick animation.
- **The tick glyph draws once** where a confirmed tick exists — a 180ms stroke-dash draw on first reveal, then static. ⚠️ Today there are **no confirmed ticks**, so in this mockup the behaviour exists and never fires. Build it anyway; it is for the day the client sends the grid back filled in.
- **Scroll reveals** — fade + 12px rise, 400ms, ease-out, 60ms stagger across a row. Calm.
- **Before/after slider** — a smooth draggable handle, hairline-framed. ⛔ **No saturation, contrast or gloss shift on drag** (that is Glaze's matte→gloss device). The image simply reveals.
- **Service rows** — on hover the row's background lifts to `--chalk` and the hairline darkens to `--rule-strong`, 120ms. ⛔ No tilt, no 3D, no shadow bloom, no scale on the row itself. Contained images scale 1.02 max, 220ms.
- **Area chips** — `--ink-wash` fill on hover, 120ms. Twenty chips, so the motion has to be cheap and quiet.
- **Buttons** — `--ink` → `#000` fill on hover, 120ms. ⛔ No lift, no shadow, no colour shift.
- ⛔ **NO COUNT-UPS.** ⚠️ **And specifically: ⛔ do not animate the 4.9 or the 257.** They are not page figures in this build at all (B9), and the moment a number counts up on this site the next one gets invented. "20 areas" and "13 services" are the only countable facts and they render as static text.
- **The `[CONFIRM]` cells reveal with everything else** and are ⛔ **never** animated separately, highlighted, pulsed, shimmered or made to look pending. A gap sits there plainly; it does not perform.
- **Always respect `prefers-reduced-motion`** — no hairline draw, no matrix stagger, no tick draw, no scroll reveals, no row hover lift, no chip transition. The before/after slider still drags (it is a control, not an animation) but loses its easing. Everything renders in final state, immediately.

**Gradients: essentially none, and that is the point.**
- ⛔ **No section gradients. No photo scrims** — the photography is high-key and contained in hairline frames, so type never sits on an image and never needs a scrim.
- The only permitted soft fill in the entire build is **`--ink-wash` at 5%**: behind the MOST REQUESTED column, behind the active area chip, and as the map coverage field.
